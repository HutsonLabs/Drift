# Drift 26.10

Drift for Mac, iPhone and iPad connects to GNOME computers, and setting one up now takes one
command on your Mac. Drift Host, the Mac host, is retired.

- Drift Host is retired. To reach a Mac from another Mac, use macOS Screen Sharing. Existing
  Drift Host installs keep working but get no updates, and Drift no longer shows paired Macs:
  those saved connections are removed.
- Set up a Linux machine from your Mac: `host/drift-gnome-setup user@machine` installs GNOME
  Remote Desktop, gives the account a desktop that runs without anyone logged in, opens the
  firewall, and opens the connection in Drift already filled in, with the machine's certificate
  trusted. The password goes to the clipboard, never into a link.
- `drift-connect:` links and QR codes: scan the code the setup prints with your iPhone or iPad
  to open the same filled-in connection in Drift there. Nothing is saved until you click Save.
- Drift for iPhone and iPad: live desktop, keyboard, mouse, trackpad and Pencil input, touch
  modes, an iPhone layout with an on-screen trackpad and key row, clipboard, and a session
  switcher.
- GNOME menus in the iPad menu bar: Session, Keys and Activities (Activities Overview, Show
  Apps).
- iPhone and iPad layouts that fit every screen: the connection form is a full-screen sheet on
  iPhone with one field per row, the toolbar fits narrow screens with a Search button, a tap on
  a connection connects and a long press opens its menu, buttons are at least 44 points, fields
  no longer zoom the page when you tap them, and the iPhone key row shows every key in two rows.
- A new app icon on Mac, iPhone and iPad, with Liquid Glass on macOS and iOS 26 or later.

## What to Test

Set up a Linux machine with GNOME from a Mac with `host/drift-gnome-setup user@machine`: Drift
should open with the connection filled in; paste the password, Save and connect to the desktop.
Scan the QR code the setup prints with your iPhone's Camera app: Drift should open with the same
connection filled in; connect, and try typing, the trackpad, scrolling, copy and paste, and
switching sessions. On iPhone, try the key row in portrait (two rows: Super, Activities, Esc,
Tab, arrows, keyboard) and landscape. On iPhone, add and edit a connection: every field should
fit, and tapping a field should not zoom the page. Tap a connection to connect, and press and
hold one for its menu. On iPad, try Activities Overview and Show Apps from the menu bar, and the
Connections window in Split View. Check that the new icon (a monitor on a navy tile) shows on
the Home Screen, in the Dock and in Finder, in light and dark mode.
